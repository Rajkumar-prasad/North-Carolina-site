Broken Garage Door Spring: Signs, Safety & What to Do

How to tell if a garage door spring is broken, why you should not fix it yourself, and what happens during a spring replacement.

Call (555) 000-0000

Springs do the heavy lifting on a garage door. A typical double door can weigh well over 100 pounds, and the springs counterbalance that weight so the opener - or your arms - only has to guide it. When a spring breaks, the door suddenly becomes very heavy and usually won't open.

Signs your spring is broken

  • A loud bang from the garage, like a firework, often when nobody was using the door.
  • A visible gap in the coiled spring above the door (torsion spring) or a stretched, snapped spring along the side tracks (extension springs).
  • The opener strains, hums or lifts the door only a few inches before stopping.
  • The door feels extremely heavy when you try to lift it by hand, or slams down quickly.
  • Loose or dangling cables beside the door.
Safety first: Do not try to wind, unwind or replace a garage door spring yourself. Springs store a large amount of energy and can cause serious injury. Keep the door closed, keep children and pets away, and don't keep pressing the opener button - it can damage the opener.

Why springs break

Most standard springs are rated for a set number of open-and-close cycles - commonly around 10,000. A household that uses the door four or five times a day can reach that in roughly six or seven years. Rust, lack of lubrication and big temperature swings also shorten spring life.

What a technician will do

  1. Secure the door so it can't move while the spring is changed.
  2. Measure the old spring (wire size, length, inside diameter) so the replacement is matched to your door's weight.
  3. Replace the spring - many technicians recommend replacing both springs on a two-spring door, since the second one is usually close to failing too.
  4. Check cables, drums, rollers and bearings for wear.
  5. Balance the door and test the opener's safety reverse.

Can I open the door with a broken spring?

It is best not to. Without the spring, the full weight of the door is on you or the opener. If a car is trapped inside and you have no other option, have at least two adults lift slowly and evenly, and prop the door securely - but calling a technician is the safer choice.
