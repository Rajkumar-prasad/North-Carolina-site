Noisy Garage Door: What the Sounds Mean

Squeaking, grinding, rattling or banging - what each garage door noise usually points to and which ones need a technician.

Call (555) 000-0000

Garage doors make some noise, but new or louder sounds usually mean a part is wearing out. Catching it early can prevent a bigger repair later.

Common noises and their causes

  • Squeaking or squealing: dry rollers, hinges or bearings. A garage-door-specific lubricant on hinges and roller bearings often helps. Don't grease the inside of the tracks - it attracts dirt.
  • Grinding: worn rollers, bent tracks, or worn opener gears.
  • Rattling: loose nuts, bolts and brackets, or a loose opener chain.
  • Popping or banging: springs under strain, or panels flexing. A single very loud bang often means a spring has broken.
  • Scraping: the door rubbing on the track or frame because something is out of alignment.

What you can do

  • Lubricate hinges, roller bearings and springs with a product made for garage doors.
  • Tighten loose bolts on the door hinges and track brackets - but never loosen anything attached to the springs or the bottom cable brackets.
  • Clean the tracks with a cloth.
Leave springs and cables alone. If the noise comes from the springs, cables or bottom brackets, call a technician. These parts are under high tension.

When a tune-up makes sense

If the door is still noisy after basic care, a professional tune-up usually covers replacing worn rollers, adjusting the springs and balance, checking cables and tightening hardware.
